## Template #1: The Personal Story

### Formula

```
I used to [struggle / mistake].

Most people [common approach]. I did too.

Then I realized [key insight].

Now I [new approach]. Results: [specific outcome].

The lesson: [one key takeaway].
```

### Example

> I used to spend 6 hours per LinkedIn post.
>
> Most people use generic templates. I did too.
>
> Then I realized: people don’t care about perfect posts. They care about REAL stories.
>
> Now I write posts in 20 minutes. My engagement is 3x.
>
> The lesson: authenticity beats polish.

### Why it works

People connect with vulnerability. A clear before-and-after makes the story believable and relatable.

### Best for

Building personal brand, showing transformation, establishing thought leadership.

### Tips

- Use specific numbers, not vague words like “many” or “lots.”
- Make the struggle real.
- Show a clear before and after.
- End with one actionable insight.

## Template #2: The Question Hook

### Formula

```
[Surprising question]?

Most people say [common answer].

But the real answer is [counterintuitive truth].

Here’s why:
- [Reason 1]
- [Reason 2]
- [Reason 3]

What would you say?
```

### Example

> Does working 80-hour weeks make you successful?
>
> Most people say yes.
>
> But the real answer is NO.
>
> Here’s why:
> - Burnout kills productivity
> - Sleep deprivation ruins decisions
> - You miss what matters (family, health)
>
> The most successful people I know? They work 20–30 focused hours a week.
>
> What would you say?

### Why it works

Questions spark immediate engagement. A contrarian answer invites discussion.

### Best for

Thought leadership, community building, driving conversations.

### Tips

- Ask a question that challenges common wisdom.
- Give 2–3 clear reasons for your take.
- End with an open question so people comment.

## Template #3: The Mini-Thread

### Formula

```
[Number] mistakes I made [doing X]:

1) [Mistake 1]
→ [How I fixed it]

2) [Mistake 2]
→ [How I fixed it]

3) [Mistake 3]
→ [How I fixed it]

4) [Mistake 4]
→ [How I fixed it]

5) [Mistake 5]
→ [How I fixed it]

Avoid these. You’ll save [time / effort / money].
```

### Example

> 5 mistakes I made building my email list:
>
> 1) I sent too many emails
> → I switched to 1 email a week. Opens doubled.
>
> 2) I wrote salesy subject lines
> → I switched to curiosity-driven subjects. Opens tripled.
>
> 3) I sent from a corporate email
> → I switched to a personal email. Response rate 5x.
>
> 4) I only sold my product
> → I started sharing free tips. Sales quadrupled.
>
> 5) I ignored analytics
> → I tracked opens, clicks, and conversions. Revenue 10x.
>
> Avoid these. You’ll save 2 years of learning.

### Why it works

People love learning from mistakes. The format is scannable and highly shareable.

### Best for

Educational content, establishing expertise, giving practical tips.

### Tips

- Use real metrics.
- Make the mistakes relatable.
- Show cause and effect clearly.
- Promise a time or effort saving.

## Template #5: The Trend Take

### Formula

```
[News / Trend] is happening.

What most people think: [common take]

What I think: [contrarian take]

Why: [reasoning]

This matters because [impact]
```

### Example

> Everyone’s talking about ChatGPT.
>
> What most people think: “It will replace writers.”
>
> What I think: “It will replace bad writers.”
>
> Why: AI is great at generating content. It’s terrible at creating truth.
>
> This matters because: writers who can think critically will thrive. The rest will be automated.

### Why it works

Timely topics get shared. Contrarian takes spark debate.

### Best for

Trending topics, unique perspective, viral potential.

### Tips

- Reference a current trend.
- Go against conventional wisdom, but back it up.
- Provide solid reasoning.
- Make it shareable.

## How to customize any template for your niche

1. Pick ONE template.
2. Replace the bracketed placeholders with your own facts.
3. Add one personal detail or number.
4. Remove any fluff.
5. Read it out loud. If it sounds robotic, rewrite it.

The templates are starting points. Your voice is the differentiator.
